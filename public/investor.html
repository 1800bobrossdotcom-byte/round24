01 — THE TANGLE

The books say one thing. The field did another. Nobody can prove which.

How it works today

  • × Pay logs in one spreadsheet, rent roll in another, P&L in a third — none of them tied together.
  • × Labor gets split across buildings by hand, in cell comments, weeks after the work.
  • × Payroll reconciled from memory and margin notes — no audit trail, and hours that never land on a building at all.
  • × Cost per unit is a guess, so pricing, staffing, and renewals are guesses too.

How Caliper does it

  • → One ledger. Every timer, receipt, lease, and building expense in the same system.
  • → Hours self-allocate to the doors they were spent on — in the field, as they happen.
  • → A true per-building P&L that reconciles automatically, the moment a timer closes.
  • → Encrypted, role-scoped, and mobile-first — for the crew who never had software.
02 — THE PRODUCT

One connected system, from the timer in the field to the P&L per door.

Five surfaces, one ledger. Everything below is built and running in production today.

2.1

Measured labor

the hour, captured true

Field timer

Geofenced clock-in, live presence, and pause-and-resume across jobs — a priority call or a multi-day job never loses a minute. Verified on-site.

Voice work orders

Dictate a repair from the field — intent inferred even in a noisy job site, photo required.

Split-day allocation

An operator divides one day across the doors they worked — self-serve, no back-office guessing.

Timesheets

Crew self-log on a week planner; the office sees per-operator and per-property master tallies.

2.2

the number nobody had

Dollar-weighted allocation

Per-building P&L

Rent, labor, materials, and fixed overhead resolved to one statement — per building, automatically.

Salary dispersion

A fixed salary spread across the doors actually worked, by hours — no lump of overhead.

Allocated vs. unallocated

06 — The market

Fragmented, spreadsheet-bound, and priced per door.

The wedge is labor — the largest controllable cost in maintenance, and the one nobody can currently see per door. Caliper prices to the door, so the model is the market: every door under management is a unit of revenue.

◱Who it's for

Small-to-mid property-management companies that run their own in-house maintenance crews — past the spreadsheet, below the enterprise platforms built for institutional multifamily.

$Priced per door

Published, not quoted. Nobody in this category posts pricing; Caliper does — set below the labor leakage the allocation engine surfaces in the first month.

⟋Entry point

The pain begins where the spreadsheet breaks — the moment an operator can no longer hold the allocation in their head. That threshold is the qualifying question and the first filter.

Where it expands

  • →Per-seat, upward. Crew seats land the account; office and owner seats carry the margin.
  • →White-label. A management company resells Caliper to the owners it serves — a channel, not a customer.
  • →Portfolio. Those owners are the same buyer, one layer up, with the same unanswered question.
  • →The benchmark. Once enough shops run the ledger, Caliper knows what a job should cost — the one asset in this category that compounds.

Why now

  • ·Crews carry smartphones — the field is finally instrumentable.
  • ·Labor is the cost line under the most pressure and the least measured.
  • ·Systems of record own the books but treat the field as an afterthought, leaving the operational ledger open.

Market framing is our own bottom-up view of the category — illustrative, not a forecast.

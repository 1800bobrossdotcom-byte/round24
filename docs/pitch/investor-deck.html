01 — The tangleProblem

The books say one thing. The field did another. Nobody can prove which.

Rent is tracked to the dollar. Labor — the largest controllable cost in the building — lives in pay logs, texts, and memory. When an owner asks what a unit actually cost to maintain, the honest answer is a guess.

How it works today

  • ×Pay logs in one spreadsheet, rent roll in another, P&L in a third — none of them tied together.
  • ×Labor gets split across buildings by hand, in cell comments, weeks after the work.
  • ×Payroll reconciled from memory and margin notes — no audit trail, and hours that never land on a building at all.
  • ×Cost per unit is a guess, so pricing, staffing, and renewals are guesses too.

How Round24 does it

  • →One ledger. Every timer, receipt, lease, and building expense in the same system.
  • →Hours self-allocate to the doors they were spent on — in the field, as they happen.
  • →A true per-building P&L that reconciles automatically, the moment a timer closes.
  • →Encrypted, role-scoped, mobile-first — for the crew who never had software.

How CAM works

A building's measured operating cost — labor plus materials — is allocated pro-rata by rentable square footage across its commercial tenants, then trued up against the CAM each was billed. Per-tenant, branded, print or PDF.

08 — The marketModel

Fragmented, spreadsheet-bound, and priced per door.

Round24 prices to the door, so the model is the market: every door under management is a unit of revenue, and the wedge is the cost line every one of those doors already carries.

◱ Who it's for

Small-to-mid property-management companies that run their own in-house maintenance crews — past the spreadsheet, below the enterprise platforms built for institutional multifamily.

$ Priced per door

Published, not quoted. Nobody in this category posts pricing; Round24 does — set below the labor leakage the allocation engine surfaces in the first month.

⟋ Entry point

The pain begins where the spreadsheet breaks — the moment an operator can no longer hold the allocation in their head. That threshold is the qualifying question, and the first filter.

Where it expands

  • →Per-seat, upward. Crew seats land the account; office and owner seats carry the margin.
  • →White-label. A management company resells Round24 to the owners it serves — a channel, not a customer.
  • →Enterprise. Office parks and towers are the same buyer, one layer up — and a CAM reconciliation only measured labor can defend.
  • →The benchmark. Once enough shops run the ledger, Round24 knows what a job should cost — the one asset in this category that compounds.

Market framing is our own bottom-up view of the category — illustrative, not a forecast.
